Sample Name: Followup - Medication Access and Cardiovascular Risk

Medical Specialty:

Consult - History and Phy.

Description: Follow-up for multiple medication refills and cardiovascular risk counseling amid a recent change in housing and insurance coverage.

(Medical Transcription Sample Report)

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient reports confusion about numerous medicines and difficulty maintaining prescriptions after separation from his household, loss of insurance coverage, and relocation. He is out of Plavix and wishes to clarify its continued indication. A recent colonoscopy was reported as reassuring. He continues smoking approximately three-quarters of a pack daily despite known cardiovascular disease, including a prior stroke and concern for previous myocardial ischemia. He reports occasional low blood glucose readings in the 60s, which he treats with candy. He is experiencing considerable family and housing-related stress and obtains some psychiatric medication through a separate prescriber. Cardiology follow-up is overdue.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY: Prior cerebrovascular event, cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and gastrointestinal ulcer or bleeding history discussed in relation to antiplatelet therapy.

SOCIAL HISTORY: Recently changed residence and lost part of his insurance coverage. Active tobacco use.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Medication reconciliation. Renew eligible chronic therapies and clarify dispensing arrangements after the patient's move. Continue specialist prescribing for psychiatric medications.
2. Antiplatelet therapy. Discussed ongoing need for Plavix in the context of vascular disease and gastrointestinal bleeding risk; clinician planned coordination with the gastrointestinal service and prescribing specialist.
3. Cerebrovascular and cardiovascular risk. Strong smoking cessation counseling; patient expresses willingness to quit. Arrange cardiology follow-up.
4. Diabetes with episodic low glucose readings. Continue home monitoring and review dosing. Reinforce ophthalmologic assessment.
5. Gastrointestinal protection. Omeprazole was changed to pantoprazole during medication review. Follow up in approximately four months.
