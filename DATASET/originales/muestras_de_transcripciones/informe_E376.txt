Sample Name: Evaluation of Arm Pain and Paresthesia

Medical Specialty:

Consult - History and Phy.

Description: Persistent unilateral arm pain and electrical sensations, with chronic lumbar spinal stenosis.

(Medical Transcription Sample Report)

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient returns with persistent intermittent discomfort in one arm, described as electrical pain and perceived swelling. The symptoms began several weeks after a fall in which the arm or shoulder struck a wall. They are provoked by certain arm positions, grooming, and shaving and may improve when the neck is turned or the arm is repositioned. He reports no associated loss of strength. Previously ordered electrodiagnostic testing was canceled and has not yet been rescheduled. He also has established lumbar spinal stenosis with back pain on standing or walking and relief after movement or changes in position. Acetaminophen provides limited symptomatic benefit.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY: Lumbar spinal stenosis and chronic back pain.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: Upper extremity strength was preserved and comparable bilaterally on grip, pushing, and pulling. Shoulder movement did not consistently reproduce pain. Position-related symptoms raised concern for irritation of a peripheral nerve or cervical nerve pathway; the precise site was not determined.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Unilateral arm pain with neuropathic features. Reschedule the previously canceled nerve conduction/electrodiagnostic study. The cause may involve neural irritation after the fall, but localization and diagnosis are uncertain. After discussion with the supervising clinician, begin low-dose desipramine at bedtime for neuropathic pain, advising that relief may take one to two weeks and that sedation is possible. Continue tolerated acetaminophen for breakthrough pain.
2. Lumbar spinal stenosis. Chronic symptoms were reviewed. Continue conservative management while tolerable; invasive options were discussed only in general terms.
3. Medication renewals were arranged where needed.

Return in approximately two months to review testing and response to treatment.
