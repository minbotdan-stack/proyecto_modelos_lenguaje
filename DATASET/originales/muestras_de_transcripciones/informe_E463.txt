Sample Name: General Medicine Followup - Prostate Biopsy and Hypertension

Medical Specialty:

Consult - History and Phy.

Description: Review of an indeterminate prostate biopsy, thrombocytopenia with hepatic findings, and blood pressure management.

(Medical Transcription Sample Report)

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient presents for followup after a prostate biopsy and recent laboratory work. He was informed that the tissue showed cells suspicious for adenocarcinoma but that a definitive cancer diagnosis was not established in the explanation given at this visit. Urology documentation suggested repeat PSA and biopsy in approximately one year, while the patient recalls being told to return in six months. He also has persistent thrombocytopenia under evaluation, with reports of an enlarged spleen, hepatomegaly, and fatty liver. Previous alcohol use may contribute, but the etiology has not been settled. A liver biopsy has been discussed but not completed. Morning systolic blood pressure at home is often 140 to 150. Most other complaints are stable; previously bothersome joint pain has improved following therapy.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY: Hypertension, coronary disease by history, thrombocytopenia, hepatosplenomegaly with fatty liver, and prostate abnormality under surveillance. The patient relates a history of major abdominal surgery and prior myocardial events.

MEDICATIONS: Hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg, lisinopril 10 mg, metoprolol 25 mg, and other chronic prescriptions.

LABORATORY DATA: Hemoglobin A1c approximately 6.1, previously in the 5% range. Platelets remained low. Cholesterol was regarded as satisfactory.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Indeterminate prostate biopsy suspicious for malignancy. Continue Urology followup and clarify the interval for repeat PSA and possible biopsy; avoid labeling the finding as proven prostate carcinoma.
2. Thrombocytopenia with splenomegaly and fatty liver. Continue specialist evaluation. The clinician advised followup with the liver service about whether the previously considered biopsy is required.
3. Hypertension with elevated morning readings. Increase lisinopril from 10 to 20 mg daily; continue hydrochlorothiazide and metoprolol.
4. Mild increase in A1c. Review diet and continue metabolic monitoring.
5. Medication reconciliation and appropriate prescription renewals were completed. Future colonoscopy surveillance was discussed.

Return for routine followup in approximately six months.
