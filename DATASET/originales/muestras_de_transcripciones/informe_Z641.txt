Sample Name: Diabetes Followup - Foot Tingling and Hoarseness

Medical Specialty:

Consult - History and Phy.

Description: Evaluation of distal sensory symptoms and intermittent voice changes in an elderly man with longstanding diabetes.

(Medical Transcription Sample Report)

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: An approximately 90-year-old man with diabetes since 1981 reports recently developing tingling in both feet, sometimes extending into the legs, with occasional imbalance but no significant foot pain. He uses NPH insulin, approximately 18 units by his account, and monitors blood glucose. He also reports intermittent hoarseness, voice changes when speaking or singing, and irritation in the throat, with a history of suspected dust-related allergies. He denies marked difficulty swallowing. Home blood pressure readings are generally lower than those recorded in clinic, which he attributes to anxiety during visits. He takes lisinopril and hydrochlorothiazide and has a history of proteinuria. A home monitor has occasionally recorded a low pulse; prior electrocardiographic and specialist assessments were reviewed.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: Office blood pressure is elevated compared with the patient's home recordings. Feet were examined for skin condition and distal sensation. The oropharynx and cardiopulmonary systems were also examined. No obvious infected foot lesion was described.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Distal paresthesias, possibly early diabetic neuropathy. Discussed the chronic nature of diabetic nerve injury, foot protection, and glucose control. Because symptoms are predominantly numbness and tingling rather than pain, medication was deferred after discussion of limited benefit and adverse effects.
2. Intermittent hoarseness and throat irritation, possibly allergic or related to dryness. Trial of allergy treatment and nasal moisturizing measures was discussed. Avoid excessive use of decongestants that may affect blood pressure and heart rate.
3. Diabetes and hypertension. Continue established treatment, monitor home readings, and renew needed prescriptions.

Followup in approximately three months with fasting lipid studies, urine testing, and other routine laboratory monitoring.
