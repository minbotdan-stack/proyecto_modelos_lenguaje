Sample Name: General Medicine Followup - Parkinson Disease and Reflux

Medical Specialty:

Consult - History and Phy.

Description: Medication review and followup for gait imbalance, nocturnal gastroesophageal reflux, and constipation.

(Medical Transcription Sample Report)

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: An older patient with Parkinson disease presents for an extended review of medications and ongoing symptoms. Tremor is relatively controllable, but balance and walking have become more difficult. Followup with neurology and physical therapy were discussed. The patient has bothersome reflux predominantly at night, awakening around 11:30 p.m. with throat burning despite raising the head of the bed. Symptoms have been persistent for several months, with a history of antireflux surgery approximately ten years previously and recurrent symptoms for several years. He is taking several gastrointestinal medications, including omeprazole and sucralfate. Sucralfate has been associated with constipation, for which he has used polyethylene glycol, fiber, and occasional laxative therapy. He is intentionally losing weight and uses nighttime positive airway pressure for sleep apnea. He denies chest pain and significant shortness of breath during the discussion.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY: Parkinson disease, gastroesophageal reflux disease, constipation, hypertension, thyroid disease, and sleep apnea.

PAST SURGICAL HISTORY: Previous antireflux surgery, by history.

MEDICATIONS: Extensive medication reconciliation included neurologic therapy, furosemide, thyroid medication, omeprazole, sucralfate, polyethylene glycol, and melatonin. Specific doses were not consistently established in the conversation.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Refractory nocturnal gastroesophageal reflux. Stop omeprazole and begin pantoprazole twice daily, approximately 30 minutes before breakfast and dinner. Gastroenterology referral was placed for consideration of further evaluation, given recurrent symptoms despite treatment and prior surgery.
2. Parkinson disease with gait and balance complaints. Continue neurologic followup and physical therapy; no new neurologic medication adjustment was clearly established.
3. Constipation, possibly aggravated by sucralfate. Bowel regimen was discussed, including a daily stool softener and review of laxative use.
4. Chronic medical management. Medication list reviewed and refills arranged as needed. An INR blood test requested by another clinician could not be added to the earlier sample, so a new draw was ordered.

Followup in approximately six months.
