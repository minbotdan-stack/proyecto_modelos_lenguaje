TITLE: General Medicine Followup - Dysuria and Knee Pain
DESCRIPTION: Evaluation of urinary burning and frequency, intermittent knee pain following a fall, and medication-related tremulousness.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient returns with approximately two weeks of discomfort on urination and increased urinary frequency. He has chronic prostate-related symptoms and reports nocturia, recently two to three times nightly, previously more frequent while taking his diuretic at night. A companion assists with the history and medication review. The patient also reports episodic sharp knee pain after falling from a chair. There was bruising but no significant swelling, and the pain is intermittent rather than consistently related to weight bearing. A companion describes episodes of marked shaking while he holds a cup. The patient uses albuterol approximately once daily and takes Depakote; he also drinks coffee and sometimes takes medication without breakfast. He was not shaking during the visit. Medication lists included duplicate prescriptions, although the clinician determined that these did not necessarily represent double dosing.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY: Chronic urinary or prostate symptoms, chronic knee complaints, and additional conditions treated with albuterol and Depakote. The patient reports using CPAP.

MEDICATIONS: Medication reconciliation was performed. Albuterol and Depakote, reportedly 1500 mg at night, were discussed. Other medications were renewed as needed.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Dysuria and urinary frequency. Urinalysis and urine testing were requested. Because of the clinical suspicion for urinary infection, an empiric 10-day antibiotic course was prescribed. The patient was instructed to complete the full course and return if symptoms persisted after treatment.
2. Intermittent knee pain following a fall. No convincing fracture was identified clinically; a contemplated x-ray was not pursued. Tylenol as needed was prescribed for discomfort.
3. Episodic tremulousness. Potential contributors include albuterol, caffeine, and taking medication without food. The cause was not definitively established. Regular meals and taking appropriate medications with food were discussed.
4. Elevated blood glucose readings. Blood work including glycemic assessment, electrolytes, blood counts, and lipids was arranged. Diabetes treatment would depend on the results; a new diagnosis or medication was not confirmed during this discussion.
5. Chronic conditions and medication management. Prescriptions requiring renewal were addressed, with review of duplicate entries.

Laboratory testing was to be completed after the visit, with telephone notification if treatment changes were required. Followup in six months, or earlier if the urinary symptoms failed to improve.
