TITLE: Hypertension Followup
DESCRIPTION: Routine follow-up for elevated blood pressure and antihypertensive medication adjustment.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: A 77-year-old patient returns for hypertension follow-up. The blood pressure is elevated despite taking the available prescribed medicine today. Medication review suggests that an earlier second agent may have been discontinued when blood pressure was low and the patient experienced dizziness. The patient is currently taking one antihypertensive although two had previously been prescribed. Repeat measurements are still mildly elevated. The patient does not report new headaches, visual changes, weakness, or leg swelling. Routine aspirin use and influenza vaccination are noted.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: Repeat blood pressure remains above the desired range but is lower than the initial reading. The legs are without concerning new swelling. Heart and lungs are described as satisfactory during examination.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN: Hypertension with suboptimal control on the present regimen. A combination antihypertensive tablet containing hydrochlorothiazide and another agent was arranged for delivery by mail. The patient is to continue the current medication until the replacement arrives, then discontinue the prior tablet and use the new medication as instructed. Blood pressure recheck and laboratory testing, including a fasting lipid panel, are planned in one month. The patient was reminded to fast overnight rather than for excessively prolonged periods and to drink water to avoid dehydration. Routine follow-up in six months.
