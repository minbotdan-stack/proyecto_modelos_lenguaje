TITLE: Acute Cough and Shoulder Pain
DESCRIPTION: Evaluation of productive cough with occasional blood streaks and chronic shoulder discomfort.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: A 53-year-old patient reports approximately one week of sinus congestion and cough productive of yellow mucus with occasional small streaks of blood. Coughing is accompanied by brief lightheadedness and headache. Symptoms are improving, without ongoing fever or resting dyspnea. He has been using cold medicine and leftover cough syrup and notes secondhand smoke exposure. He separately describes chronic shoulder pain, intermittently reaching 7/10, worsened by push-ups and weight training. He was involved in a motor vehicle accident in 2006 requiring prolonged hospitalization. He reports weight reduction from approximately 316 to 288 pounds through a weight program and declines vaccination at this visit.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: Lungs were examined without a significant abnormality described. Shoulder motion and strength are substantially preserved, without a convincing indication of major acute structural injury.

IMPRESSION AND PLAN: Resolving upper respiratory infection or bronchitis, likely viral. Prescribed guaifenesin for symptomatic relief and advised avoidance of secondhand smoke. Antibiotics were not indicated on this assessment. Shoulder discomfort appears musculoskeletal; radiographs were initially considered, then deferred after discussion with the supervising clinician given reassuring motion and examination. Seek reassessment if symptoms persist or worsen. Continue planned endocrinology follow-up and routine primary care review in approximately nine months, sooner if necessary.
