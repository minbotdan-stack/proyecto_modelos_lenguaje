TITLE: General Medicine Followup - Vitamin D Deficiency
DESCRIPTION: Followup of low vitamin D, occasional low heart rate, prior syncope, and osteoarthritis.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient returns doing generally well. An echocardiogram obtained at the end of January was normal by the clinician's review. He occasionally records a pulse around 52 at home and is concerned because he takes atenolol. He had a brief episode of syncope requiring hospital observation in February, which he associates with overheating; he has not described recurrent associated symptoms at this visit. Most recorded heart rates have been in the 60s and 70s. His blood pressure and lipid values were regarded as controlled. He has disabling knee osteoarthritis limiting walking distance and needs paperwork for renewal of his disability parking permit. A low vitamin D level was found on recent testing.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY: Hypertension or cardiovascular risk treated with atenolol, prior syncopal episode, knee osteoarthritis, and vitamin D insufficiency. History of colonoscopy with removal of polyps and continuing urology followup.

MEDICATIONS: Atenolol; over-the-counter vitamin D, approximately 800 units daily, before the new regimen.

LABORATORY DATA: Lipid results discussed included LDL 67. Vitamin D was borderline low. Other recent laboratory findings were not described as requiring a change in management.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Low vitamin D. Prescribe a concentrated vitamin D supplement once weekly for 12 weeks. Suspend the patient's routine supplement during this treatment and resume it after completion. Repeat vitamin D testing shortly before followup.
2. Intermittent relative bradycardia and prior syncope. The echocardiogram is reassuring. Continue low-dose atenolol if asymptomatic; with recurrent low pulse associated with dizziness or presyncope, hold the dose and seek timely reassessment.
3. Hypertension and lipid management. Continue existing therapy. Aerobic activity such as a stationary bicycle was recommended, given limited walking tolerance from knee pain.
4. Bilateral knee osteoarthritis. Disability parking documentation was reviewed.
5. Continue urology surveillance as scheduled.

Followup in approximately four months, after laboratory re-evaluation.
