TITLE: General Medicine Followup - Diabetes and Medication Reconciliation
DESCRIPTION: Follow-up for diabetes, congestive heart failure, chronic kidney disease, and difficulty obtaining medication refills.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient returns primarily because of persistent difficulty obtaining refills through the pharmacy. Several medications have not arrived despite repeated requests, and he brings multiple bottles for review. He has not taken isosorbide dinitrate for approximately two to three months after running out. He continues hydralazine and other cardiovascular medications. He initially declines restarting the nitrate, partly because he wishes to use medication for erectile dysfunction, but agrees to consider taking it after discussion of its role in his heart failure regimen. He has not had new lower-extremity swelling. He also requests replacement nasal spray for nighttime congestion.

Regarding diabetes, he uses approximately 12 to 14 units of insulin at bedtime. He reports glucose falling into the 40s by morning unless he begins the night with a reading near 200 or eats overnight; fasting readings may otherwise be around 90 to 108. He is reluctant to add mealtime injections. Intermittent constipation and bleeding with straining are also discussed.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY: Congestive heart failure, diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease with nephrology follow-up, anemia treated through nephrology, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and erectile dysfunction.

MEDICATIONS: Extensive reconciliation included insulin, hydralazine, isosorbide dinitrate, amlodipine, metoprolol, diuretics including Lasix, simvastatin, aspirin, gabapentin, pancreatic enzymes, iron, calcium with vitamin D, docusate, and senna. Availability and adherence vary because of refill difficulties.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: The patient is conversant. He denies current leg swelling, and recent podiatry examination was satisfactory. Heart and lungs were examined, without a clearly documented abnormal finding in the conversation.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Medication access and adherence. Medications were reviewed individually and refills renewed. Discussed the importance of continuity, particularly of the heart failure regimen. Isosorbide dinitrate was reordered during discussion, although the patient's willingness to continue it had fluctuated.
2. Diabetes with reported nocturnal hypoglycemia. Record fingerstick glucose three to four times daily for two weeks. Pharmacist visit in two weeks to review readings and adjust insulin as indicated; do not infer safe control from isolated fasting values.
3. Erectile dysfunction. Discussed the danger of sildenafil with nitrates and the concerns related to his cardiac status; sildenafil was not prescribed.
4. Nighttime nasal congestion. Flunisolide nasal spray prescribed.
5. Follow up in approximately three months, with an earlier pharmacist review.
