TITLE: Primary Care Followup - Toenail Deformity
DESCRIPTION: Chronic painless toenail deformity and transition from nursing home care to independent living.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient requests Podiatry assessment for a markedly thickened and disfigured toenail present since approximately 2001. He denies associated pain but is troubled by its appearance and recurrent growth after cutting. He expects to leave his nursing facility soon and needs to arrange pickup rather than mailing of his chronic medications until a stable address is established. He has hypertension and hyperlipidemia controlled on medication. He reports current cigarette use, approximately half a pack daily for 38 years, and abstinence from alcohol for about 20 months.

MEDICATIONS: Hydrochlorothiazide, a cholesterol medication, aspirin and other maintenance therapies reviewed for renewal.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: Blood pressure is well controlled. Toenail markedly abnormal, consistent with onychomycosis; no appreciable pain at the nail base.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Onychomycosis/abnormal nail: Refer to Podiatry. Discussed that prolonged oral antifungal therapy may be needed and requires periodic liver monitoring; no definitive systemic course initiated today.
2. Hypertension/hyperlipidemia: Refill existing medications for pharmacy pickup; hydrochlorothiazide dose to be checked against the prior supply.
3. Tobacco use: Smoking cessation counseling and nicotine gum discussed and ordered with instructions for safe use.
4. Transition from nursing home: Arrange medication pickup within the pharmacy's specified period.
5. Preventive care: Reviewed influenza vaccination, colonoscopy options, and pending eye appointment. Patient may defer colonoscopy while considering screening.
