TITLE: Hypercalcemia and Exertional Dyspnea Followup
DESCRIPTION: Evaluation of persistent postnasal drainage, possible parathyroid disease, exertional dyspnea, and hypertension.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: A patient in his early 60s complains of persistent postnasal drainage following dental extractions, intermittent nasal congestion, and progressive shortness of breath with activity including walking and intercourse. He formerly smoked for approximately 25 to 30 years but quit over a decade ago. Prior pulmonary function testing was normal, and prior chest imaging, echocardiography, and stress testing did not identify a definite explanation; he feels exercise tolerance has worsened since those tests. His blood pressure has required medication adjustment, and he wishes to pursue a lower sodium diet and weight reduction. He also has unexplained hypercalcemia and has been told there may be a parathyroid disorder. A previously requested 24-hour urine collection has not yet been completed. He reports loss of muscular endurance and intermittent leg weakness.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: The clinician hears a possible heart murmur, although a previous echocardiogram did not clearly confirm significant valve pathology. Lungs examined without a definitive new abnormality.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Hypercalcemia, possible primary hyperparathyroidism. Complete ordered serum and 24-hour urine studies before choosing medical or surgical treatment.
2. Exertional dyspnea of uncertain cause despite prior reassuring pulmonary and cardiac testing. Repeat echocardiography and seek cardiology evaluation, with additional testing determined by subsequent findings.
3. Hypertension and obesity. Maintain medication adherence and follow low-salt diet, gradual exercise, and weight reduction plan.
4. Postnasal drip. Resume prescribed allergy treatment, including cetirizine, and monitor symptoms.
5. Provide a clear written medication list and follow up in several months.
