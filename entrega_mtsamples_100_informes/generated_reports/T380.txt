TITLE: Hospital Followup - Diabetes and Lower Extremity Infection
DESCRIPTION: Posthospital assessment of leg infection and severe hyperglycemia with confusion about insulin dosing.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient returns following hospitalization for lower extremity infection and poor glucose control. He describes taking Lantus insulin in several daytime and nighttime doses, approximately 10, 10 and 30 units, and subsequently adding newly prescribed insulin using a similar pattern. Blood sugar readings remain markedly elevated, including 292, 402, and 394 mg/dL, with intermittent meter alerts for ketones. He continues glipizide while metformin was held at discharge. Leg swelling has improved after antibiotics, although pain persists. He takes a diuretic and other chronic cardiovascular medications. He also requests renewal of medical supplies.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: Lower extremity erythema and warmth have improved; no fever is noted. Blood pressure is reasonably controlled. Lungs without acute concerning findings.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Poorly controlled diabetes: Reconciled long- and short-acting insulin schedules and emphasized that these preparations are not interchangeable. Following attending review, a specific insulin dose was increased to 15 units with instructions to update the home regimen. Continue glucose logging and contact clinic for adjustments rather than making overlapping changes. Further dosing specifics require reference to the written discharge/medication instructions.
2. Recent lower extremity infection: Clinical improvement after clindamycin; complete prescribed course and monitor for recurrence.
3. Renal dysfunction: Check chemistry and kidney function today, particularly given persistent hyperglycemia and medication changes.
4. Edema and cardiovascular conditions: Continue indicated medications and reconcile refills.
5. Supplies: Renewal arranged; coordinate follow-up with established primary care clinician.
