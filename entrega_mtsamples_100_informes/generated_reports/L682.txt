TITLE: Persistent Balanitis and Medication Followup
DESCRIPTION: Follow-up of recurrent foreskin inflammation with discharge, tearing of the eye, and prescription refill difficulties.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient returns with his partner because of persistent inflammation of the glans and foreskin after a prior single-dose antifungal treatment. Pain and difficulty retracting the foreskin have improved, but pronounced redness, superficial irritation, and yellowish discharge persist or recur. Both partners describe recurrent fungal problems and have undergone treatment. He also reports episodic excessive tearing and foreign-body sensation in one eye, especially with wind, bright light, or television viewing, without a visible lesion on the current examination. Weight has recently risen over the holidays and he is working to lose the additional pounds. There are difficulties obtaining refills of antihypertensive and lipid medications.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: Genital examination reveals significant erythema with persistent irritation and exudate; appearance is worse than previously despite symptomatic improvement. The eye shows no distinct visible abnormality explaining tearing.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Persistent presumed balanitis, incompletely responsive to oral antifungal therapy. Repeat a single oral antifungal dose and refer to urology for assessment of persistent lesions and possible need for further investigation or foreskin procedure. No definitive alternative diagnosis or malignancy was established.
2. Excess tearing and intermittent foreign-body sensation. Trial lubricating eye drops; consider ophthalmology review if persistent or progressive.
3. Medication access. Renew appropriate antihypertensive and simvastatin prescriptions with pharmacy pickup as requested.
4. Continue weight reduction efforts and return in approximately four months or sooner if genital symptoms worsen.
