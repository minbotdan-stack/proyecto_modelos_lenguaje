TITLE: Hypertension and Chronic Pain Followup
DESCRIPTION: Followup for poorly controlled hypertension and chronic severe lower extremity pain with medication adherence difficulties.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient returns with persistent severe pain and aching in the feet and legs, sometimes involving the great toe, with associated cold sensation and pain disturbing sleep. He has been taking several 500 mg over-the-counter analgesic tablets at a time without adequate relief. He requests resumption of tramadol after a prior interruption in treatment and acknowledges difficulty keeping within prescribed dosing intervals. He describes considerable financial and marital stress. There is uncertainty regarding consistent use and timely refilling of antihypertensive medications.

MEDICATIONS: The medication list was reviewed, including antihypertensive therapy, hydrochlorothiazide, a cholesterol medication, terazosin, omeprazole, gabapentin, and analgesic agents. Actual adherence is uncertain.

SOCIAL HISTORY: The patient describes lack of income, difficulty obtaining food, marital discord, and past substance use.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: Repeat blood pressure is approximately 160/90. The patient continues to describe cold, aching feet.

IMPRESSION AND PLAN:
1. Hypertension, inadequately controlled, with concern for inconsistent medication use. The patient was instructed to obtain the prescribed blood pressure medications today and take them regularly. Nursing blood pressure reassessment was requested in two weeks, with further review in approximately four weeks and consideration of another antihypertensive if elevation persists.
2. Chronic lower extremity pain. Tramadol was ordered for use at prescribed intervals, approximately every eight hours, with a short initial supply and the remainder to follow by mail. He was strongly advised to discontinue excessive additional Tylenol use. Other pain medications and their effects were reviewed; he was asked to report inadequate relief rather than adjust doses independently.
3. Laboratory studies and urine testing were requested. Radiographic and ultrasound examinations were also discussed and arranged for subsequent assessment.
