TITLE: Primary Care Followup - Chronic Pain and Hypertension
DESCRIPTION: Opioid medication review in a patient with chronic pain, hypertension, and ongoing substance use concerns.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient returns after Pain Clinic follow-up for renewal of morphine prescribed for chronic pain. He describes intermittent abdominal discomfort and sinus symptoms. The clinician notes that a 30-day morphine prescription has lasted approximately three months, raising questions about the frequency of use. The patient describes taking regular and breakthrough medication variably depending on pain severity. Previous urine toxicology was positive for cocaine. The risks of concurrent cocaine and prescribed opioids and requirements of the controlled medication program were reviewed. Diabetes is managed without current hypoglycemic medication following previous use of glyburide; reported glucose measurements have generally been reasonable. The patient continues to smoke. His blood pressure remains elevated after repeat measurement.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: Repeat blood pressure approximately 150/90.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Chronic pain on morphine: Reviewed differences between long- and short-acting analgesics and the reported variable use. One further limited prescription was provided, with instructions not to take unnecessary doses and to contact clinic before supplies run out. Strict avoidance of cocaine and urine toxicology monitoring emphasized.
2. Hypertension: Increase lisinopril to 40 mg daily and begin low-sodium diet. Nurse blood pressure check in three weeks.
3. Tobacco use: Discussed cessation and nicotine lozenges, including avoidance of smoking while using them.
4. Sinus symptoms: Trial saline nasal spray.
5. Diabetes: Continue dietary management and routine monitoring.

Urine testing requested. Return in three months.
