TITLE: Gout, Renal Insufficiency, and Medication Followup
DESCRIPTION: Follow-up for gout and chronic joint symptoms, with concern about nephrotoxicity from longstanding indomethacin use.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient returns for medication renewals and reports anticipated knee replacement, deferred for personal circumstances. He has longstanding gout and has used indomethacin for approximately 15 years, despite prior discussions about impaired renal function. Last available creatinine was approximately 1.7. A reduced-dose allopurinol regimen and colchicine were introduced previously, yet he continues indomethacin out of concern about recurrent severe attacks. Several other prescriptions, including nasal steroids, lipid-lowering therapy, metoprolol, omeprazole, and thyroid replacement, require reconciliation and renewal. Blood pressure is mildly high at presentation. A prior antihypertensive, valsartan or losartan, is no longer on the active list.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY: Gout, chronic renal insufficiency, hypertension, dyslipidemia, chronic knee disease, and thyroid disease after partial thyroid surgery.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Gout with chronic use of indomethacin and renal impairment. Extensive counseling on progressive kidney risk. Agreed to reduce indomethacin to every other day, then potentially every third day as tolerated, eventually reserving it for short courses during flares. Continue other prescribed urate-lowering and prophylactic therapies with renal considerations.
2. Renal insufficiency. Repeat creatinine and metabolic laboratory tests before further long-term NSAID exposure.
3. Medication refills. Renew appropriate maintenance therapies; do not restart a previously discontinued angiotensin-receptor blocker without review.
4. Check uric acid, thyroid tests, CBC, and other monitoring laboratories. Follow up after laboratory results and at the next routine visit.
