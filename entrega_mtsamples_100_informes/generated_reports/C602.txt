TITLE: Resistant Hypertension Followup
DESCRIPTION: Persistent intermittent blood pressure elevation despite multiple medications, with consideration of secondary causes.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient returns with his home blood pressure log and concerns about office measurements. He is currently treated with four antihypertensive agents and reports generally more favorable readings at home and at outside visits; recent outside values were approximately 128 to 135 systolic. In the office, systolic readings were reported at approximately 165 to 170. He is uncertain whether a previously proposed magnetic resonance angiogram of the renal arteries was already completed elsewhere. Renovascular disease has been raised as a possible secondary cause of difficult-to-control hypertension. He continues to smoke approximately six to eight cigarettes daily. He acknowledges longstanding snoring severe enough that his wife sleeps in another room, although he does not report substantial daytime sleepiness.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY: Hypertension requiring multiple drugs. Dyslipidemia and glucose measurements were reviewed and described as well controlled.

MEDICATIONS: Four blood pressure medications, including clonidine for which additional refills were requested; the complete regimen was not specified consistently.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: Repeat clinic systolic blood pressure was approximately 170. No chest pain or shortness of breath was reported.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Difficult-to-control hypertension with variable home and clinic readings. Continue medication management and review the prior outside workup. A renal artery MRA will not be repeated until the clinician verifies whether it has already been done. The patient will request outside records or notify the clinic if no study exists.
2. Snoring and possible obstructive sleep apnea. A sleep study through the Pulmonary service was ordered as a potential investigation for a secondary contributor to hypertension.
3. Tobacco use. Smoking was reviewed and reduction or cessation encouraged.
4. Medications. Clonidine refill and other necessary renewals were addressed.

Followup in six months, or earlier if records or symptoms warrant further evaluation.
