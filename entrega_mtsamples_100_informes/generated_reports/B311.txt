TITLE: General Medicine Followup - Low Blood Glucose
DESCRIPTION: Evaluation of symptomatic low home blood glucose readings in an older patient taking diabetes medication.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: An 86-year-old gentleman with diabetes and hypertension presents because he suspects that his diabetes medication is causing fatigue and dizziness. He has taken it inconsistently and forgot it during a six-day trip. While off treatment, home glucose readings were reportedly in the 90 to 101 range. After taking his medication, he recorded glucose as low as 49. He has been modifying his diet to reduce starches and is concerned about whether he still requires diabetes treatment. He continues to take several antihypertensive medicines but is uncertain of some names and doses. He also discusses erectile difficulties and asks whether these may be related to a blood pressure medication. He describes recent cold symptoms and has a scheduled eye clinic appointment. Previous operations on both eyes were reported. He has poor dentition and is reluctant to obtain dental care.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY: Diabetes mellitus and hypertension. History of ophthalmic surgery.

MEDICATIONS: Metformin, taken intermittently before this visit; multiple antihypertensives, incompletely identified.

SOCIAL HISTORY: Former smoker, quit approximately 15 years previously.

REVIEW OF SYSTEMS: Dizziness and tiredness temporally associated by the patient with his diabetes tablets. No chest pain or dyspnea was elicited during the visit.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Diabetes mellitus with reported hypoglycemia. Given repeated low readings including a value of 49, the patient was repeatedly instructed to stop metformin for now. Repeat laboratory testing was ordered, with further medication decisions to be based on the results. The importance of addressing symptomatic low glucose was discussed.
2. Hypertension. Medication review was performed and required prescriptions renewed; the patient was advised to continue the other agreed medications.
3. Erectile difficulties. The patient requested medication and expressed concern about antihypertensive side effects. No new treatment for erectile dysfunction was clearly documented.
4. Eye and dental care. Continue scheduled ophthalmology followup. Dental assessment and possible dentures were recommended in view of poor dentition.

The patient was asked to obtain laboratory testing and collect prescriptions, with further followup to be arranged according to results.
