TITLE: General Medicine Consultation - Persistent Cough
DESCRIPTION: Prolonged nonproductive cough after upper respiratory illness, with reflux and possible reactive airway component.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient reports a persistent dry, hacking cough beginning approximately one month ago after a cold. Symptoms were initially severe enough to cause vomiting and sometimes disrupted sleep but have partially improved. He has been taking over-the-counter omeprazole for known gastroesophageal reflux, as well as nasal spray and other symptomatic medicines, with some relief. He is a lifelong nonsmoker. He describes increased coughing in cold air and has had similar episodes previously. A remote positive tuberculosis exposure test and treatment during military service were discussed; the present illness has not otherwise been suggestive of active tuberculosis. He denies recent persistent fever, chest pain or significant dyspnea.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY: Gastroesophageal reflux disease and remote treatment associated with positive tuberculosis screening.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: No audible wheeze with examination. Oropharynx without striking inflammation. No systemic findings suggesting acute serious illness.

IMPRESSION: Persistent cough following upper respiratory illness, possibly reactive airways or cough-variant asthma. Reflux may contribute; etiology is not definitive.

PLAN: Trial albuterol inhaler with spacer as needed, monitoring clinical response. Continue reflux management as appropriate. Chest radiography was considered initially but, after discussion with supervising physician, was not ordered at this visit. Return in approximately one month for reassessment, sooner if symptoms worsen.
