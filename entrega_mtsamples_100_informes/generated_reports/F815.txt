TITLE: General Medicine Followup - Diabetes and Hypertension
DESCRIPTION: Medication reconciliation and supply renewals in a patient with diabetes, renal disease, and prior surgeries.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient returns with a companion to review his insulin regimen, blood pressure, and extensive prescription supplies. Home glucose and blood pressure are recorded through a remote-monitoring system. He reports using NPH insulin about 5 units twice daily and varying aspart doses, often closer to 10 units with meals rather than the listed 5 units. He has large remaining supplies of NPH but needs aspart, test strips, and selected equipment or medications. Lisinopril had previously been stopped because of lightheadedness. Clinic blood pressure is approximately 133/61 after an initially higher reading. He has chronic kidney disease, hearing impairment treated with hearing aids, glaucoma and other eye disease, prior left hip replacement, and a history of prostate surgery and urinary or ostomy supplies. His dizziness has improved.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY: Insulin-treated diabetes, chronic kidney disease, hypertension or elevated blood pressure, hearing impairment, glaucoma, hip arthritis status post replacement, and prior genitourinary surgery.

MEDICATIONS: NPH and aspart insulin, aspirin, vitamins, and other chronic medicines and medical supplies.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: Blood pressure 133/61. No specific new acute abnormality was documented on routine examination.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Diabetes. Review variable insulin use and avoid inadvertent oversupply. Renew aspart and glucose testing supplies and obtain followup laboratory studies. Continue glucose logs through the home system.
2. Blood pressure and chronic kidney disease. Restart lisinopril at a reduced dose of 2.5 mg daily rather than the prior 5 mg because of previous dizziness. Reassess tolerance and renal monitoring.
3. Glaucoma and diabetic eye screening. Arrange Ophthalmology followup; current appointment status was uncertain.
4. Complex medication and equipment needs. Renew requested prescriptions for later use but ship only supplies currently required, including selected insulin and testing materials. Review ostomy or urinary drainage supplies.
5. Preventive care. Vaccinations were discussed and reported recently received.

Followup in several months.
