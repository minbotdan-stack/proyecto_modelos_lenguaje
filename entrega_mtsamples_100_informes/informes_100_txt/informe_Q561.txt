Sample Name: General Medicine Followup - Diabetes and Hypertension

Medical Specialty:

Consult - History and Phy.

Description: Review of diabetes therapy after medication intolerance and routine blood pressure follow-up.

(Medical Transcription Sample Report)

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient returns for routine follow-up. Blood pressure is in the 150s in clinic, which he attributes partly to being upset; he reports values in the 130s at home. He has diabetes and describes hospitalization after substantial urinary frequency and dehydration while receiving a higher dose of metformin, approximately 850 mg. Previously used insulin was discontinued by another clinician. He reports tolerating a lower metformin dose more comfortably. He checks his glucose intermittently and has fingertip discomfort from frequent finger sticks. He is status post coronary bypass and cholecystectomy. Colonoscopy in 2010 was reportedly normal. He continues smoking and reports greatly reduced alcohol use.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY: Type 2 diabetes, hypertension, coronary artery disease status post bypass surgery, and prior cholecystectomy.

MEDICATIONS: Metformin at a reduced tolerated dose; other chronic medications reviewed. Stool softener use was discussed.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Diabetes mellitus: Continue tolerated therapy pending assessment of glycemic control. Check hemoglobin A1c and fasting laboratory studies. Reviewed intermittent home glucose testing and finger-stick technique.
2. Hypertension: Elevated office readings versus lower home values. Continue monitoring and reassess.
3. Hyperlipidemia/cardiovascular risk: Obtain fasting lipid profile and reinforce tobacco cessation.
4. Health maintenance: Prior colonoscopy without reported precancerous findings. Seasonal influenza immunization discussed; patient elected to return for vaccination. Arrange Eye Clinic follow-up.
5. Medication reconciliation: Clarified that stool softeners and other medicines should be identified correctly before sharing or substituting products.

Patient to return for fasting studies. Abnormal results will be communicated.
