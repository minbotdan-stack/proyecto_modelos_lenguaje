Sample Name: General Medicine Consultation - Shoulder and Elbow Pain

Medical Specialty:

Consult - History and Phy.

Description: Musculoskeletal complaints and medication reconciliation in a patient with prior cardiac surgery.

(Medical Transcription Sample Report)

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient presents for review of laboratory results, medication refills, and intermittent left shoulder and elbow pain rated approximately 4 to 5/10. Symptoms interfere with sleeping on that side and with certain reaching and arm movements. He reports chronic exertional discomfort or burning after walking short distances despite prior coronary evaluation and open heart surgery; Cardiology has not identified a definite cause. He wishes to address weight and physical conditioning before additional investigation. He is also concerned about irritated areas of skin and the cost and location of filling numerous prescriptions.

LABORATORY DATA: CBC and metabolic testing are reassuring, with creatinine 1.04. Triglycerides 293, total cholesterol 188, HDL 37, LDL 92. Liver studies reported normal.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: Shoulder motion provokes discomfort with specific maneuvers. Elbow findings were discussed as compatible with an overuse/tendinous process. Skin shows dry irritated scratched areas.

IMPRESSION AND PLAN:
1. Shoulder and elbow pain: Findings suggest mechanical or tendinous pain, including lateral epicondylar irritation. Discussed conservative treatment. Patient prefers observation rather than steroid injection at this time.
2. Hypertriglyceridemia: Reviewed diet and prior niacin intolerance due to flushing; no new lipid medication initiated on current findings.
3. Exertional discomfort with prior cardiac work-up: Symptoms remain incompletely explained. Patient prefers to work on weight reduction before pursuing more aggressive reassessment.
4. Dry skin: Moisturizing skin care advised.
5. Chronic medications: Extensive reconciliation and renewal for preferred pharmacy and 90-day dispensing.

Return as needed if symptoms persist or worsen.
