Sample Name: General Medicine Followup - Cardiovascular and Renal Artery Findings

Medical Specialty:

Consult - History and Phy.

Description: Follow-up for cardiovascular medication renewals, prior hematuria, and a reported small right renal artery aneurysm.

(Medical Transcription Sample Report)

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient reports generally feeling well and obtaining care from several outside specialists. Evaluation for hematuria included cystoscopy and imaging, reportedly showing a small aneurysm of the right renal artery. Urologic examination and PSA were reassuring by the patient's account. Cardiology reviewed cholesterol, reported at approximately 207, without a full lipid panel available. He checks his blood pressure periodically and reports satisfactory values. A prior squamous cell carcinoma of the ear was removed, and he follows with dermatology at six-month intervals. He reports daily walks of approximately 25 minutes. There is no smoking or alcohol use. His medications are approaching expiration, and recent outside laboratory records are not available to the current clinic.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY: Cardiovascular disease under outside cardiology care, hypertension, dyslipidemia, prior hematuria, reported right renal artery aneurysm, and squamous cell carcinoma of the ear. History of diverticulosis on previous colonoscopy is reported.

MEDICATIONS: Cardiovascular drugs include Altace and Lipitor, reported as 80 mg daily for the latter; other existing prescriptions were reviewed.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: Blood pressure was satisfactory at the visit. General examination including oral, pulmonary, and cardiovascular assessment was conducted without a specific abnormality recorded in the dialogue.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN: Obtain laboratory testing for medication monitoring, including electrolytes and thyroid studies, and renew appropriate maintenance prescriptions. Obtain documentation of the recent outside lipid studies or repeat fasting lipids before renewing Lipitor as required by clinic policy. The patient was advised to provide copies of laboratory reports from outside clinicians to prevent unnecessary repeat testing. The significance and possible management of the small renal artery aneurysm were discussed in general terms, with management dependent on size and specialist assessment; no operation was arranged. Continue specialty follow-up and routine primary care review, approximately annually.
