Sample Name: Medication Reconciliation and Followup

Medical Specialty:

Consult - History and Phy.

Description: Review of multiple medication bottles, diabetes control, dizziness, and sleep-related psychological symptoms.

(Medical Transcription Sample Report)

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient brings numerous medication bottles because he is unsure which drugs are current. He fills a weekly medication organizer and wishes to discard obsolete or duplicate prescriptions. During reconciliation, previously instructed changes to hydrochlorothiazide-containing therapy, metformin dose reduction, and potassium discontinuation were reviewed. Home glucose readings are often in the 120s without symptomatic hypoglycemia; previous hemoglobin A1c was approximately 5.3%. He also describes sleep disruption, nightmares, unexplained crying, and longstanding distress associated with past military experiences. He prefers individual rather than group counseling. Recent intermittent dizziness and imbalance raise concern for low blood pressure while taking multiple antihypertensive agents; a prior reading was approximately 96/54. Sleep apnea and nocturia further disturb sleep.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY: Type 2 diabetes mellitus, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, sleep apnea, and longstanding psychological symptoms or possible trauma-related distress.

MEDICATIONS: Reconciliation confirmed approximately eight regularly used tablets including aspirin, metformin, metoprolol, lisinopril, terazosin, lipid therapy, and the current combination antihypertensive regimen. Old potassium and magnesium preparations were reviewed for discontinuation, along with duplicates and topical drugs of uncertain current need.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Polypharmacy and medication confusion. Clarified each active medication, marked the bottles, and identified outdated or duplicate prescriptions for removal from current use.
2. Diabetes, tightly controlled. Continue the reduced metformin regimen and monitor for hypoglycemia.
3. Intermittent lightheadedness with occasional low blood pressure. Review antihypertensive burden and consider further reduction if readings or symptoms warrant.
4. Insomnia, nightmares, and emotional distress. Arrange individual mental health assessment for possible trauma-related symptoms. Continue evaluation of sleep apnea and CPAP adherence.
5. Continue routine surveillance and follow-up.
