Sample Name: Chronic Knee Symptoms and Spine Disease - Followup

Medical Specialty:

Consult - History and Phy.

Description: Evaluation of knee instability and spasms with prior spine imaging, hypertension, and questions about analgesic therapy.

(Medical Transcription Sample Report)

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS: The patient returns because of persistent symptoms involving the knees, described more as spasms or abnormal movement than constant pain. A previously requested knee MRI was apparently canceled and was not performed; a cervical spine MRI was completed instead. He has a history of back surgery and reports some back discomfort, particularly at night. Current blood pressure is satisfactory on amlodipine. He does not regularly take aspirin. The patient would like ibuprofen but expresses reluctance to use multiple medications. Prostate screening and follow-up with urology were reviewed; his preferences regarding further prostate evaluation remain to be clarified with that service. Breathing has improved, and he reports no cigarette use for approximately 25 years.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY: Chronic back symptoms after spine surgery, knee complaints, hypertension, and prior urinary tract infection.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION: The lower extremities were examined, including range of motion and straight-leg elevation. Symptoms appeared more consistent with intermittent spasms or mechanical complaints than acute severe joint pain. Blood pressure was acceptable today.

ASSESSMENT AND PLAN:
1. Bilateral knee symptoms. Reorder MRI of both knees and arrange orthopedic consultation after the imaging. The patient was given the scheduling sequence in writing.
2. Intermittent musculoskeletal pain. Trial of salsalate before ibuprofen, with both prescriptions provided but explicit instruction not to take both concurrently. Use only as necessary and with food because of gastrointestinal and other medication risks.
3. Hypertension. Continue amlodipine and monitor if dizziness occurs.
4. Urologic follow-up. Re-establish consultation to clarify the patient's screening and management preferences.
5. Follow up in approximately three months and telephone notification if imaging is markedly abnormal.
